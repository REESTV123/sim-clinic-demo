neffy product card

Use only these facts in the call. Anything else is a Medical Information request (1800 642 865).

Indication and dosing

  • Adrenaline nasal spray for emergency treatment of anaphylaxis in adults and children aged 4 years and older.
  • 1 mg for patients 15 kg to <30 kg; 2 mg for patients ≥30 kg. Pack of two single-dose sprays.
  • Prescribe with an anaphylaxis management plan, which includes an ASCIA Action Plan for Anaphylaxis.

Unmet need

  • Fewer than 50% of patients received adrenaline before hospital (Australian ED audit).
  • Barriers: not carrying the device, needle anxiety, hesitation and lack of confidence in the moment.

Efficacy

  • Recognised as being as effective as adrenaline auto-injectors (e.g. EpiPen) by the TGA, PBAC and ASCIA. All adrenaline devices approved in Australia work rapidly to treat anaphylaxis.
  • Rapid changes in HR and BP from 1 minute, comparable to 0.3 mg IM adrenaline and auto-injector. PD study in healthy volunteers; HR and BP are surrogate markers and their clinical meaning in anaphylaxis is unclear.
  • Effective absorption regardless of nasal congestion or allergic rhinitis.
  • Oral food challenge study: 100% of patients achieved symptom resolution after one dose; improvement started within 5 minutes.

Use

  • HOLD (do not test-spray), PLACE in the nostril without angling, PRESS the plunger firmly until it clicks.
  • Call an ambulance. If no improvement after 5 minutes, give a second dose with a new spray, ideally in the same nostril.
  • 100% of trained adults administered it without error (n=42).

Safety and storage

  • Well tolerated; no serious adverse events in clinical trials. Most common: nasal discomfort and headache. No needle-related injuries.
  • Shelf life from manufacture: 1 mg 2 years, 2 mg 2.5 years. Store below 25°C; short excursions up to 50°C permitted.
  • Do not freeze. A frozen device will not work; thaw for at least one hour.

Compliance

  • Report any possible adverse event you hear about to CSL Seqirus, following company process.
  • No claims that neffy is faster, stronger or better than EpiPen or IM adrenaline. The approved word is comparable.
PBS eligibility

PBS listed, Authority Required, for anticipated emergency treatment of anaphylaxis.

  • Continuing supply: GPs can prescribe to eligible patients previously prescribed an authority prescription for adrenaline.
  • Initial supply: patients assessed to be at significant risk of anaphylaxis by, or in consultation with, a specified specialist, or discharged from hospital or ED after treatment with adrenaline for anaphylaxis.

Check the full criteria at www.pbs.gov.au.
